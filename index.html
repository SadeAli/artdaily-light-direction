$ man light-direction

A hidden sun lights the form. Drag the dashed ring to say which direction the light comes from, and the corner arc to say how far it is tilted toward you — bottom of the arc means the light rakes across from the side, top means it shines straight out of the screen at you. A small preview of the form under your light appears beside it as you aim, so you can match the picture instead of guessing a number. Then lock it in. Six forms per round.

Scoring: the 3D angle between your sun and the real one — within 3° is perfect, 45° off scores zero. Arrow keys aim (shift = coarse), enter locks, and on a phone a double-tap on the form locks without reaching for the button.

The cast shadow is honest: it is the form projected along the real light vector onto the floor, so a long shadow means a low sun and a tight pool means an overhead one — read it as a second cue.

Studio tip: find the brightest spot, then find the shadow edge — the line where the light stops and the dark side begins. Painters call that line the terminator, and the reveal draws it for you on the round forms. The light sits on the line running from the shadow edge through the brightest spot and onward.
